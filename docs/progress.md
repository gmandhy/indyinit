Implemented:

- `src/lib/coordinates.ts`: distinct normalized image, model-world, source-pixel, and display-pixel contracts; nonsquare source mapping; contain-fit forward/inverse transforms with optional one-time mirroring; letterbox clicks return absence; backing dimensions account for DPR without changing logical collision coordinates. Canvas labels are drawn in display space and stay readable.
- `src/lib/biomechanics.ts`: clamped unsigned world-landmark flexion, index PIP/DIP estimates and explicitly named MCP proxy, thumb reach ratio, Gram–Schmidt orthonormal palm basis, maximum corresponding-axis orientation deviation, strict >18° lock / <14° unlock hysteresis, and time-based display smoothing. Raw PIP estimates remain separate from the smoothed display value. Malformed arrays, nonfinite math, degenerate axes/segments, and invalid clocks throw descriptive `MathInputError`s.
- `src/lib/calibration.ts`: bounded stable-open-palm collection over >=3000 capture-time ms and >=45 valid observations; robust independent medians for `palmWorld` (model meters) and `palmPixels` (source pixels); frozen world and image bases. Window restart/rejection reasons cover absence, invalid/stale input, gaps, changed hands, small projected palms, low-confidence/out-of-frame observations, closed fingers, wrist drift, world/image-axis changes, and scale variation. A representative observed orthonormal basis is selected near the scale medians rather than averaging axes into an invalid basis. Accepted calibration does not adapt during a trial.
- `src/lib/spatialTargets.ts`: finite reachable target paths, source-pixel circles translated with the current image wrist using frozen calibrated image axes, required-landmark collision, continuous capture-time dwell, exit-to-rearm, and multi-target advancement only after release. Loss/staleness/lockout clears dwell; no repeated score from holding a target or replaying a frame. A valid observed exit is required after an interruption. Hand switch, major scale change, or explicit geometry invalidation requires recalibration. Attempts, blocked attempts, and orientation lockout events are separate; a continuous lockout consumes at most one blocked attempt, including a lock that coincides with exit.
- `src/engine/sessionReducer.ts`: browser-independent injected-monotonic-clock lifecycle with guarded transitions, cancellation/reset/stop/fault recovery, a 3000 ms countdown and fixed 15000 ms trial window, and capture-window checks with a separate bounded 250 ms delivery grace. Tick delays do not extend the trial. Loss, hidden tab, and mode changes invalidate calibration and abort active work. Actual desk fusion and late-event consumption remain phase 5–6 work.
- `src/engine/sessionController.ts`: tested engines connected to the existing replaceable stubs/replay boundary. Raw geometry stays in a frame/target buffer; ordinary snapshots notify at 10 Hz, while lifecycle/error transitions notify immediately. An independent UI-owned clock tick detects stalled input even when no new frame arrives. Expected absent/invalid data clears estimates; specific math errors become visible invalid states. Reset, stop, selection changes, and disposal clear state and reject old callback generations. Live adapters remain explicitly refused; desk calibration remains explicitly unavailable.
- Calibration schema now requires an orthonormal source-image basis in addition to the world basis; configuration revision is `geometry-2`. The base schema/test fixtures were updated together. Source/display brands and world-only angle functions have compile-time misuse assertions.
- Replay now supplies 60 bounded seconds of 20 Hz artificial source observations. The same calibration/target engines compute its angles and counters; fixture records contain no success oracle. Canvas uses the shared mirrored contain-fit transform and DPR-aware backing size. Minimal labels/readouts and README instructions were updated; both stub compositions remain interchangeable. Validated summary/ROM aggregation/export is still phase 6.

### Implemented scope

CPU fallback stays in the worker and is visibly capped at a **50 ms / 20 Hz** minimum interval; that cap does not meet the >=24 Hz inference gate. No main-thread fallback or unsupported-browser success is claimed.

Additional/duplicate frames never allocate or queue bitmaps. rVFC deduplicates media timestamps; rAF fallback deduplicates `video.currentTime`. Sequence and capture time survive the transport unchanged. Main-clock capture metadata is used when supplied; otherwise presentation/observed-video timestamps are explicitly labeled frame-time proxies. Worker timestamps are strictly increasing; unexpected sequence/timestamp responses fault visibly. Results older than 120 ms are discarded while their age remains in diagnostic statistics.
- Ownership covers camera tracks, video source/listeners, rVFC/rAF cancellation, abortable local-asset preparation, landmarker close, worker disposal acknowledgement/termination, and bitmap close on transfer failure, worker success/failure, and late creation after stop. Initialization/result deadlines (30 s / 5 s) report failure; the 1 s teardown deadline terminates a hung worker. These are bounded failure/cleanup policies, not timing corrections. Each Strict Mode effect creates a separate owner; late callbacks cannot update a replacement controller.
- `src/adapters/live.ts` connects actual samples and actual camera dimensions to the unchanged geometry/calibration engines. Tracking starts as soon as devices are ready; calibration does not restart the stream. Loss, stale input, invalid math, hide, hand/scale changes, and faults clear estimates/calibration appropriately. Reset releases live resources and returns to idle. Live exercise scoring remains disabled until phase 4; the phase 2 synthetic trial is preserved.

Video is reflected once inside its saved transform; skeleton points are mapped once; text stays in display space. Canvas/render samples stay outside React. Ordinary snapshots publish at 10 Hz; repeated invalid math is also throttled, with lifecycle/error transitions immediate.
