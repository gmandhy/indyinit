# Table tap setup

Open **Session → Tracked trial → Set up desk taps**. The microphone detects sound timing; the camera supplies the fingertip location. One microphone does not locate a sound on the desk independently. **Set up desk taps** now selects **Left / right pads**. If already in desk mode, choose that option under **Desk layout**. Single ring remains available. The built-in synthetic replay has one fixed ring; select Live camera for the two-pad setup. Changing layouts clears calibration and stops the microphone, so choose the layout before requesting it.

The task card stays beside the camera (above/below it on narrow screens). Each task shows **Task complete** only after controller checks pass. Select **Confirm and continue** to move on. Restart setup clears confirmations and calibration. Camera tracking failures return to visual calibration while preserving a healthy microphone check; microphone/device failures return to the microphone task when needed. Low-level controls remain under **Advanced desk controls**.

1. On the camera card, start the camera and allow permission. Confirm completion. On the microphone card, start the microphone separately and keep quiet until it reads **ready** (at least 1.5 seconds of continuous quiet). Confirm completion.
2. Fix the camera at an oblique angle showing both fingertip lift and desk contact. On the palm task, select **Calibrate palm / begin setup** and hold your hand still and fully in view for three seconds. Desk setup checks stable palm scale/orientation, not whether every inferred finger angle is below 30°. A noisy DIP estimate such as 41° does not block this desk step; the angle remains displayed as an estimate. Confirm the accepted result.
3. Click the LEFT side of the mirrored video under the resting index fingertip. Confirm ring placement, then follow the rest task and confirm its completion. On the practice task, record and save each of 3–5 lifts and returns. Hold still for three camera observations before saving. Finish visual calibration and confirm the completed task. The app saves Left and prompts for Right: move your hand to the RIGHT, click under the resting fingertip there, and repeat rest plus 3–5 cycles. Keep the rings separate; reversed or overlapping pads and overlapping lift paths are rejected. If lift paths overlap, separate the pads across the observed lift direction or adjust the camera angle. Each pad stores its own measured contact geometry and resting wrist position.
4. Record and save 3–5 separate timing taps alternating Left and Right, spaced at least 600 ms apart. Wait at least 250 ms after contact before saving each pair. Finish timing calibration and confirm completion. Avoid speech and other impacts while recording a pair.
5. The final card shows **Setup complete** / **Setup confirmed**. Start the fused trial, wait for the countdown, and tap inside either calibrated ring for 15 seconds. Lift/release between taps. Accepted taps flash only the matched pad and leave a marker at the latest accepted camera position. Left/Right counts and the last detected side appear in the table tap panel. Moving between pads clears pending visual contact; lift again at the destination. Travel or a sound outside the pads does not count.

The table tap panel shows microphone level, sound count, camera candidate count, accepted count, per-pad counts, last matched side, and the last accepted source-image pixel location. Left/Right mean screen sides in the mirrored video; the exported summary retains the combined tap count, without per-pad history. Video markers use the existing mirrored/letterboxed transform; pixel readouts use the original camera image. A sound without a camera candidate is never assigned a location or accepted. Setup event counts are diagnostic; starting a trial clears them.

If microphone permission or calibration fails, retry it in Controls. **Desk / kinematic-only** explicitly uses camera motion without sound confirmation. Selecting another sensor mode clears calibration and stops the microphone. Stop/reset releases devices. Raw audio remains inside the local processing path; contact coordinates stay in memory and are absent from summary exports.

## Implementation and evidence

- Normal render-clock startup settling no longer triggers a false clock-discontinuity failure. The clock mapping settles throughout quiet calibration, then freezes for matching. Drift after readiness still invalidates capture. Palm setup preserves completed quiet calibration.
- Skipped input quanta during startup restart the full quiet interval. Gaps after readiness, missing input, clipping, noise failures, and suspension remain explicit errors.
- The single-ring camera detector carries the contact extremum's position through matching and accepted feedback, rather than using a later frame or the ring center.
- The production browser regression supplies synthetic audio through a real Web Audio media stream and the built AudioWorklet. Replay tests verify matching, exactly-once counts, positions, export, and recovery. These are software evidence, not physical microphone/desk accuracy.

The clock distinction follows the [Web Audio specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio/#dom-baseaudiocontext-currenttime): rendering time advances in audio blocks and is distinct from other system clocks. Paired taps still measure this setup's audio/visual offset.

The two-pad extension has deterministic alternating-pad tests in both event arrival orders and a production browser test covering both calibrations, alternating taps, between-pad noise rejection, side counters, and trial completion. Inference/audio events in that test are artificial, while audio.spec.ts separately exercises the actual worklet.

Live acceptance remains pending: check 20 deliberate taps and 20 noise/no-contact trials on the demo hardware, then three full fused trials; record misses, false matches, microphone/browser, and camera angle. Existing performance and palm-calibration gates also remain pending.
